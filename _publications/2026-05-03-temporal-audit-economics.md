*Volume II of the trilogy "Temporal Economics" (《经济学的时间的研究》).*

Where Volume I established the formal theory, Volume II turns the temporal lens onto the existing body of economic thought. It conducts a systematic **temporal audit** of resource-based economics — including standard frameworks of production, allocation, equilibrium, and growth — asking the same question of each: *under what temporal conditions does this model implicitly hold, and what happens when those conditions are violated?*

The book examines several domains:

- **Production and allocation** — the temporal assumptions hidden inside seemingly atemporal optimization;
- **Equilibrium and adjustment** — the gap between formal convergence and the temporal feasibility of adjustment paths;
- **Growth and accumulation** — the structural conditions under which resource-based growth ceases to be temporally realizable;
- **Coordination under high-frequency conditions** — the breakdown of standard coordination mechanisms as the time scale of economic interaction compresses.

The audit identifies a recurring pattern: many results in resource-based economics depend on tacit temporal premises that are not stated, not tested, and increasingly not satisfied by the systems being modeled. The volume thereby clears the ground for the unified temporal framework that the trilogy as a whole develops.
